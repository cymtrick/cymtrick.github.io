About Adversarial Minds

Adversarial Minds is a set of long-form notes on secure and trustworthy machine learning, written by P. Rajan. The series covers the field from its foundations to current research: threat models, poisoning and evasion attacks, privacy attacks and the formal privacy frameworks that answer them, certified robustness, explainability, jailbreaking of language models, and algorithmic fairness.

The intended reader already knows machine learning reasonably well and wants to understand its security and privacy dimensions properly, rather than memorise definitions. The posts are technical, but every piece of mathematics is accompanied by the intuition that motivates it.

How the posts are written

Three rules hold across the series:

  1. Every claim traces to a primary source. Bracketed numbers in the text link to a reference list at the end of each post; wherever possible the reference is the paper's own page at arXiv, OpenReview, or the conference proceedings.
  2. Every chart is drawn from reported numbers. The figures are redrawn from tables and plots in the cited papers, never estimated. If a result could not be verified, it is not plotted.
  3. Disagreement is reported as disagreement. Where the field has not settled a question — what ε is "private enough", whether chain-of-thought explanations are faithful, which fairness definition is the right one — the post says so and gives the strongest case on each side.

Corrections are welcome. The source for the site, including the data behind every figure, is on GitHub.
