Prashanth Varma Dommaraju

Offensive security engineer, penetration tester and vulnerability researcher in Amsterdam. This blog is where I write up what I learned studying secure and trustworthy AI: how models are attacked, what the formal guarantees actually say, and what the numbers in the papers look like when you put them side by side.

What I do

I am an OSCP-certified offensive security engineer with a background in vulnerability research and in building large distributed systems. Since 2018 I have reported more than a hundred security vulnerabilities across major platforms, including remote code execution, SQL injection, authentication bypasses and insecure direct object references, and have been acknowledged by Google, Apple, Uber, Meta and Twitter for critical findings reported through HackerOne. I identified and helped patch CVE-2018-17082, a cross-site scripting vulnerability in PHP's Apache 2 SAPI handler caused by bucket-brigade reuse, working with the PHP security maintainer from root-cause analysis through fix validation. From 2018 to 2020 I was a vetted member of the Synack Red Team, running authorised penetration tests against enterprise targets, and I have carried out red-teaming and VAPT engagements for government bodies and private firms.

On the engineering side, I spent four years at Hammoq, first as founding software engineer and then as senior software engineer: migrating a monolith to Kubernetes, building a state-machine-driven pipeline that processed 25 million images, running real-time machine-vision APIs at 99.9% uptime, and integrating a fine-tuned LLaVA-7B model into a retrieval-augmented generation system. A MIG-based Kubernetes orchestration I designed took inference throughput on a single A100 from one to seven requests per second.

Research

My master's thesis at Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam was on structure-aware fuzzing of Kubernetes. I built a fuzzer for the Kubelet that uses custom C++ mutators on libprotobuf-mutator to generate valid, deeply nested Pod and Node objects that get past the initial validation layers, with a feedback-driven corpus strategy that reached considerably more code than string-based fuzzing, and a standalone tool for exploring the serialisation and deserialisation state space of Go-based distributed components.

The posts on this site grew out of the Secure and Trustworthy AI course in the same programme. They cover adversarial machine learning from the threat model through poisoning, evasion and privacy attacks to differential privacy, PAC Privacy, certified robustness, explainability, jailbreaking and fairness, and they are written to the standard I would want from a paper: every claim cited to a primary source, every chart drawn from reported numbers, and disagreements reported as disagreements.
